SECTION C — TPT REGIMENS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q18. How many TPT regimens are defined in the database?
A18. Four regimens: 6H, 3HP, 6Lfx, and 4R.

Q19. What drugs are used in the 3HP regimen?
A19. Isoniazid (H) and Rifapentine (P).

Q20. What is the duration and total number of doses for the 6H regimen?
A20. 6 months duration, 180 total doses (taken daily).

Q21. What is the dose frequency and total doses for the 3HP regimen?
A21. Weekly frequency, 12 total doses over 3 months.

Q22. Which regimen requires a 90% completion threshold, and which require 80%?
A22. 3HP requires 90% completion. 6H, 6Lfx, and 4R all require 80%.

Q23. What is the extended duration (days) allowed for the 4R regimen?
A23. 160 days (133% of the planned 4-month duration).

Q24. For which patient population is 6Lfx indicated?
A24. Contacts of MDR-TB / RR-TB patients whose index patient has an
     FQ-sensitive strain.

Q25. For which patient population is 4R indicated?
A25. Contacts of H-resistant, R-sensitive TB (Hr-TB) index patients.

Q26. What are the contraindications for the 3HP regimen?
A26. Pregnancy, PI-based ART, Nevirapine, TAF (Tenofovir alafenamide),
     children <2 years, and allergy to rifamycins.

Q27. Is 6H safe during pregnancy?
A27. Yes — the notes state "Safe in pregnancy."

Q28. What is the maximum adult daily dose of Isoniazid in the 6H regimen?
A28. 300 mg/day.

Q29. In the 3HP regimen, for an adult weighing 56–70 kg, what dose of Rifapentine
     is prescribed, and how many tablets per dose?
A29. 900 mg of Rifapentine, using 150 mg tablets — 6 tablets per dose.

Q30. For a child weighing 10–15 kg on the 3HP regimen, what is the Isoniazid dose
     and how many 100 mg tablets should be given?
A30. 300 mg — 3 tablets of 100 mg each per dose.

SECTION D — TBI DIAGNOSTIC TESTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q31. How many TBI diagnostic tests are catalogued?
A31. Three: TST (Tuberculin Skin Test / Mantoux), QGIT (QuantiFERON-Gold
     In-Tube), and T-SPOT.TB.

Q32. What is the positive cut-off for TST in PLHIV patients?
A32. ≥5 mm induration.

Q33. What is the positive cut-off for QGIT (QuantiFERON)?
A33. ≥0.35 IU/ml.

Q34. Which TBI tests require laboratory processing?
A34. QGIT and T-SPOT.TB (both IGRA tests). TST does not require a lab.

Q35. What is the cost category for TST vs IGRA tests?
A35. TST is "Low" cost; both IGRA tests (QGIT and T-SPOT.TB) are "High" cost.

Q36. Does BCG vaccination affect the specificity of IGRA tests?
A36. No — IGRA tests have high specificity even in BCG-vaccinated individuals.
     TST, by contrast, has low specificity in BCG-vaccinated persons.

Q37. Within what time must blood reach the laboratory for the QuantiFERON test?
A37. Within 8–30 hours of collection.

SECTION E — RISK GROUPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q38. How many risk groups are defined in the database?
A38. Ten risk groups.

Q39. Which risk groups do NOT require a TBI test before TPT initiation?
A39. PLHIV-ADL (PLHIV adults & adolescents), PLHIV-INF (HIV+ infants <12m),
     and HHC-U5 (Household contacts <5 years).

Q40. What is the TPT strategy for Household Contacts <5 years (HHC-U5)?
A40. TPT after ruling out active TB — no TBI test required. TPT must not be
     deferred in the absence of CXR or TBI test results.

Q41. What is the TPT strategy for PLHIV adults?
A41. TPT is given to all PLHIV after ruling out active TB disease via a
     4-symptom screen (cough, fever, weight loss, night sweats) ± CXR.

Q42. What regimen is prescribed for contacts of DR-TB (MDR/RR FQ-sensitive) patients?
A42. 6Lfx (Six months daily Levofloxacin).

Q43. What regimen is prescribed for contacts of H-resistant R-sensitive (Hr-TB) patients?
A43. 4R (Four months daily Rifampicin).

Q44. Which risk groups require TBI testing AND have a priority level of 2?
A44. HHC-5PLUS, IMMUNO-SUPP, SILICOSIS, DIALYSIS, PRE-TRANS,
     DRTB-HHC, and HRTB-HHC — all have priority_level = 2 and
     tbi_test_required = TRUE.

SECTION F — ADVERSE EVENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q45. Which adverse events related to Isoniazid require stopping TPT
     (stop_tpt = TRUE)?
A45. Drug-induced hepatitis, Convulsions, Pellagra, and Psychosis.

Q46. What is the management action for Isoniazid-induced peripheral neuropathy?
A46. Prescribe Pyridoxine 100–200 mg/day and consider stopping Isoniazid.

Q47. What is the clinical management when ALT is ≥5× ULN (without symptoms)
     in a patient on Isoniazid?
A47. Stop all drugs and wait for resolution before re-challenging.

Q48. Which Rifampicin adverse events require stopping TPT?
A48. Hepatitis, Thrombocytopenic purpura, Acute renal failure,
     and Haemolytic anaemia.

Q49. What is the management for Rifapentine-induced hypersensitivity / flu-like
     syndrome?
A49. Mild: continue and observe. Moderate-Severe: switch to 6H regimen.

Q50. Which Levofloxacin adverse events are classified as 'Rare'?
A50. Clostridium difficile diarrhoea, Tendon rupture (Achilles), and
     QTc prolongation / arrhythmia — all require stopping Levofloxacin.

Q51. What is the recommended action for Rifampicin-induced discolouration
     of body fluids?
A51. Reassure the patient — it is cosmetic only and not harmful.

SECTION G — DRUG-DRUG INTERACTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q52. How many drug-drug interaction records are in the database?
A52. 16 interactions (5 for Isoniazid, 11 for Rifamycin).

Q53. Does Isoniazid increase or decrease levels of Warfarin?
A53. Isoniazid increases Warfarin levels. INR should be monitored closely.

Q54. Why is Rifamycin contraindicated with Protease Inhibitors (PIs)?
A54. Rifamycin significantly decreases PI levels. Rifabutin is the
     recommended alternative.

Q55. What is the clinical advice when Rifamycin-based TPT is used alongside
     hormonal contraceptives?
A55. Use barrier contraception or DMPA while on rifamycin-based TPT, as
     rifamycin decreases effectiveness of ethinyl oestradiol / levonorgestrel.

Q56. Can Isoniazid interact with anti-epileptic drugs? Which ones?
A56. Yes — Isoniazid increases levels of Phenytoin, Carbamazepine,
     Primidone, and Valproic acid. Anticonvulsant levels should be monitored.

Q57. Which antiretroviral is acceptable to use with Rifamycin-based TPT
     (with a dose adjustment)?
A57. Raltegravir (RAL) — the dose should be increased to 800 mg twice daily
     when taken with Rifampicin.

Q58. What interaction does Rifamycin have with methadone / buprenorphine?
A58. Rifamycin decreases their levels, risking opioid withdrawal. The OST
     dose should be increased and the patient closely monitored.

SECTION H — TREATMENT OUTCOME TYPES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q59. What dose completion threshold defines "Treatment Completed" for 3HP?
A59. ≥90% of doses within 133% of the planned duration, with the person
     remaining well.

Q60. What dose completion threshold defines "Treatment Completed" for 6H/6Lfx/4R?
A60. ≥80% of doses within 133% of the planned duration.

Q61. What is the definition of "Lost to Follow-up" (LTFU) for the 3HP regimen?
A61. TPT interrupted for ≥4 consecutive weeks.

Q62. What is the LTFU definition for 6H / 6Lfx regimens?
A62. TPT interrupted for ≥8 consecutive weeks.

Q63. What does the outcome code "DISC_TOX" mean?
A63. Discontinued due to Toxicity — TPT permanently discontinued by the
     treating doctor due to adverse events or drug-drug interactions.

Q64. Under what circumstance is "Not Evaluated" (NOT_EVAL) assigned?
A64. When the outcome is not assessable — e.g., records lost or patient
     transferred without documentation.

DQ1. Describe the complete administrative hierarchy used in the PMTPT
     database to organize TB services in India, from the national level
     down to the patient. What tables represent each level, and how are
     they linked?

A-DQ1.
     The PMTPT database mirrors India's public health administrative
     structure through a chain of related tables:

     1. STATES (states table)
        The topmost geographic unit. Each state has a unique state_code
        (e.g., 'MH' for Maharashtra, 'UP' for Uttar Pradesh) and a region
        label (North, South, East, West, Central). Ten states/UTs are seeded.

     2. DISTRICTS (districts table)
        Each district belongs to a state via the state_id foreign key.
        15 districts are seeded, e.g., Mumbai and Pune under Maharashtra,
        Lucknow and Varanasi under Uttar Pradesh.

     3. TB UNITS / TUs (tb_units table)
        TB Units are intermediate administrative units that sit between the
        district and the health facility. Each TU belongs to a district via
        district_id. Eight TUs are seeded, e.g., "Lucknow TU-1" under the
        Lucknow district.

     4. HEALTH FACILITIES (health_facilities table)
        Individual service-delivery points such as HWCs, PHCs, CHCs, ART
        Centres, DR-TB Centres, and private clinics. Each facility links to
        a TU (tu_id) and a district (district_id). Facility capabilities are
        captured as boolean flags: is_tbi_testing, is_igra_available,
        is_xray_available.

     5. PERSONS (persons table)
        Individuals registered in the system — index TB patients, household
        contacts, and high-risk persons. Each person is linked to a district
        and to the health facility where they registered (registered_at).

     6. INDEX TB PATIENTS & CONTACTS
        (index_tb_patients and contacts tables)
        Index patients are notified TB cases. Their contacts are enumerated
        in the contacts table, linking a contact person to an index patient
        via index_patient_id.

     7. CLINICAL TABLES (screenings, TBI tests, eligibility, enrolments,
        dose records, follow-up visits)
        These form the patient-level care cascade, each referencing
        person_id and hf_id.

     The hierarchy thus flows:
     State → District → TB Unit → Health Facility → Person → Clinical Data.
     Foreign key constraints enforce this at every step, ensuring referential
     integrity across the entire cascade.

DQ2. Compare all four TPT regimens (6H, 3HP, 6Lfx, 4R) available in the
     database. For each, discuss the drugs used, duration, dosing frequency,
     total doses, completion threshold, key indications, and major
     contraindications.

A-DQ2.

  ┌──────────┬──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┐
  │ Regimen  │ 6H                       │ 3HP                          │
  ├──────────┼──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
  │ Drug(s)  │ Isoniazid (H)            │ Isoniazid (H) + Rifapentine  │
  │ Duration │ 6 months                 │ 3 months                     │
  │ Frequency│ Daily                    │ Weekly                       │
  │ Doses    │ 180                      │ 12                           │
  │ Threshold│ 80%                      │ 90%                          │
  │ Extended │ 239 days                 │ 120 days                     │
  ├──────────┼──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
  │ 6Lfx     │ Levofloxacin (Lfx)       │ 4R: Rifampicin (R)           │
  │ Duration │ 6 months                 │ 4 months                     │
  │ Frequency│ Daily                    │ Daily                        │
  │ Doses    │ 180                      │ 120                          │
  │ Threshold│ 80%                      │ 80%                          │
  │ Extended │ 239 days                 │ 160 days                     │
  └──────────┴──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘

  6H (Six months Isoniazid):
     The most widely used regimen, safe in pregnancy and for all age groups
     including infants >12 months. Indicated for PLHIV (adults and children),
     HIV+ infants <12 months in TB contact, household contacts <5 years, and
     most other risk groups. Maximum adult dose is 300 mg/day. Pyridoxine
     must be co-prescribed for at-risk groups to prevent peripheral neuropathy.
     Contraindicated in active TB disease, acute/chronic hepatitis, and
     allergy to isoniazid.

  3HP (Three months weekly Isoniazid + Rifapentine):
     A shorter, weekly regimen associated with higher completion rates
     compared to 6H. Indicated for persons ≥2 years who are household
     contacts of DS-TB patients and PLHIV not on PI/NVP/TAF-based ART.
     Should ideally be taken with food and under direct observation.
     Contraindicated in pregnancy, PI-based ART, Nevirapine, TAF, and
     children <2 years. Causes orange discolouration of secretions, which
     is cosmetic and harmless.

  6Lfx (Six months Levofloxacin):
     A second-line regimen used specifically for contacts of MDR-TB/RR-TB
     patients where the index patient has a fluoroquinolone-sensitive strain.
     Adult dose varies by weight: 750 mg if <45 kg, 1000 mg if ≥45 kg.
     Paediatric dispersible 100 mg tablets are available with weight-banded
     dosing. Contraindicated where FQ resistance is confirmed. Clinicians
     should monitor for Achilles tendon pain and joint abnormalities in
     children.

  4R (Four months Rifampicin):
     Indicated for household contacts of H-resistant, R-sensitive (Hr-TB)
     index patients. Maximum adult dose is 600 mg/day. Evidence in children
     0–14 years is currently limited to specific geographies for evidence
     generation only. Contraindicated in pregnancy, Nevirapine-based ART,
     and PI-based ART due to significant drug-drug interactions.

  Key comparison point: 3HP has the highest completion threshold (90%) but
  the shortest total duration (12 weeks). 6H and 6Lfx are the longest
  regimens at 6 months. 4R and 3HP both use rifamycins and therefore share
  many contraindications (pregnancy, certain ARTs).

DQ4. A new patient on Rifamycin-based TPT is also taking oral contraceptives,
     Warfarin, and Nevirapine-based ART. Using the drug_interactions table,
     explain the clinical risks for each co-medication and what actions
     should be taken.

A-DQ4.

     Rifamycin is a potent inducer of cytochrome P450 enzymes and affects
     multiple co-medications. Based on the drug_interactions seed data:

     1. ORAL CONTRACEPTIVES (Ethinyl oestradiol / Levonorgestrel)
        Effect: Rifamycin DECREASES contraceptive efficacy.
        Risk: The patient is at high risk of unintended pregnancy if relying
        on oral contraceptive pills while on a rifamycin-containing regimen
        (3HP or 4R).
        Action: Switch to barrier contraception (condoms) or DMPA
        (depot medroxyprogesterone acetate) for the duration of rifamycin-
        based TPT. Resume oral contraceptives only after completing the
        rifamycin course.

     2. WARFARIN (Anticoagulant)
        Effect: Isoniazid INCREASES Warfarin levels (risk of bleeding).
        Note: Rifamycin actually decreases Warfarin in general, but the
        database specifically records Isoniazid increasing it.
        Risk: If on a combination regimen (3HP = H + Rifapentine), there
        are opposing interactions. INR can become unpredictable.
        Action: Monitor INR closely and frequently throughout the TPT
        course. Dose adjustment of Warfarin will likely be needed. Coordinate
        with the anticoagulation team.

     3. NEVIRAPINE (NVP)-BASED ART
        Effect: Rifamycin DECREASES Nevirapine levels significantly.
        Risk: Sub-therapeutic NVP levels can lead to virological failure
        and development of HIV drug resistance — a serious clinical concern
        in a PLHIV patient.
        Action: The combination is CONTRAINDICATED. Rifampicin- or
        Rifapentine-containing regimens (3HP, 4R) must NOT be used in
        patients on Nevirapine. The recommended alternative is the 6H
        regimen (Isoniazid alone), which does not interact significantly
        with Nevirapine. The ART team should be informed and involved in
        the decision.

     OVERALL CLINICAL DECISION:
     Given the Nevirapine-based ART, the patient is ineligible for 3HP or
     4R. The only safe TPT option is 6H. The oral contraceptive interaction
     with rifamycin is therefore moot (since 6H contains no rifamycin), but
     warfarin interaction with Isoniazid must still be monitored. A multidis-
     ciplinary approach involving TB, HIV, and anticoagulation services is
     essential.

DQ5. Explain the complete supply chain structure for TPT drugs as modelled
     in the database. Describe each level, its buffer stock requirement,
     replenishment source, and how the system ensures uninterrupted supply
     to the patient.

A-DQ5.
     The supply chain is modelled in two tables: supply_chain_levels (the
     structural levels) and drug_stock (actual inventory at each facility).
     The chain has five levels, flowing from central procurement down to
     the individual patient:

     LEVEL 1 — GMSD / CMSS (Government Medical Store Depot / Central
     Medical Services Society)
        Buffer: 0 months (this is the central procurement authority).
        Role: Procures drugs at the national level through Central TB
        Division / MoHFW. Supplies the State Drug Stores.

     LEVEL 2 — STATE DRUG STORE (SDS)
        Buffer: 3 months.
        Replenishment: From GMSD/CMSS.
        Calculation: (Quarterly consumption ÷ 3) × 11, minus all lower
        level stocks. The large multiplier (11) ensures a broad buffer to
        cover the entire state's needs and accommodate delays.

     LEVEL 3 — DTC DRUG STORE (District TB Centre)
        Buffer: 3 months.
        Replenishment: Quarterly from the SDS.
        Calculation: (Quarterly consumption ÷ 3) × 8, minus existing stocks.

     LEVEL 4 — TU DRUG STORE (TB Unit)
        Buffer: 2 months.
        Replenishment: Quarterly from the DTC.
        Calculation: (Quarterly consumption ÷ 3) × 5, minus existing stocks.

     LEVEL 5 — HEALTH FACILITY STORE
        Buffer: 2 months.
        Replenishment: Monthly from the TU Drug Store.
        Reserve calculation: 2 × total TPT beneficiaries currently on a
        full course. This ensures each active patient's treatment is secured
        for at least two months even if replenishment is delayed.

     LEVEL 6 — TREATMENT SUPPORTER (Patient/Caregiver)
        Buffer: 0 (no buffer — issued per beneficiary as needed).
        The full TPT course is issued to the treatment supporter or patient
        at initiation to enable uninterrupted self-administration.

     The drug_stock table records actual inventory per health facility
     (hf_id), regimen (regimen_id), and supply chain level (level_id), with
     fields for full courses in stock, batch number, and expiry date. This
     enables real-time stock visibility and expiry monitoring at every point.

     The database seed data shows, for example, Dharavi HWC (HF-MUM-001)
     holding 25 courses of 6H and 15 courses of 3HP as of 2024-03-01, while
     Sion ART Centre holds 40 courses of 6H — the largest stock of any
     single regimen across all seeded facilities.

DQ6. Discuss how the PMTPT database supports monitoring and evaluation of
     the TB Preventive Treatment programme. Describe each of the seven
     monitoring indicators, their targets, how they are calculated using
     the indicator_reports table, and what the Q1 2024 data tells us about
     programme performance in Mumbai and Central Delhi.

A-DQ6.

     The monitoring framework uses two tables — monitoring_indicators
     (definitions and targets) and indicator_reports (actual reported data).
     The result_pct is a STORED GENERATED COLUMN computed automatically as
     (numerator ÷ denominator) × 100, ensuring calculation consistency.

     THE SEVEN INDICATORS:

     1. CONT-INV-COV — Contact Investigation Coverage (Target: 100%)
        Measures what fraction of pulmonary TB patient contacts completed
        evaluation for TB disease and TB infection. A 100% target reflects
        the goal of leaving no contact unevaluated. Reported monthly.

     2. TPT-COV — TPT Coverage (Target: 90%)
        Measures what fraction of eligible individuals actually initiated
        TPT. Tracks the conversion from eligibility to treatment start.
        Reported monthly.

     3. TPT-COMP — TPT Completion Rate (Target: 90%)
        Tracks what fraction of those who started TPT successfully
        completed it. Low completion can indicate adherence challenges,
        toxicity, or inadequate follow-up. Reported monthly.

     4. TPT-BREAK — Breakdown Rate to Active TB (No target set)
        Monitors how many TPT beneficiaries developed active TB during or
        within 24 months after completing TPT. This is a key outcome
        indicator of programme effectiveness. Reported quarterly (NULL
        target in the database — it is a surveillance metric, not a
        performance benchmark).

     5. PLHIV-TPT-COV — PLHIV TPT Coverage (Target: 90%)
        Specifically tracks TPT initiation among newly enrolled PLHIV after
        ruling out active TB. PLHIV are the highest-priority group, so this
        indicator is tracked separately. Reported monthly.

     6. U5-TPT-COMP — Under-5 HHC TPT Completion (Target: 90%)
        Tracks completion specifically for children <5 years who are
        household contacts — the most vulnerable paediatric group.
        Reported monthly.

     7. POST-TPT-FU — Post-TPT Follow-up Coverage (Target: 90%)
        Measures whether individuals who completed TPT receive scheduled
        follow-up assessments at 6, 12, 18, and 24 months post-completion.
        This is critical for early detection of TB breakdown. Monthly.

     Q1 2024 PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS (from indicator_reports seed data):

     Mumbai (District: Mumbai / HF: Dharavi HWC):
     - Contact Investigation Coverage: 18/20 = 90.00%
       → Below the 100% target. Two contacts were not fully evaluated.
     - TPT Coverage: 16/18 = 88.89%
       → Slightly below the 90% target. Two eligible individuals did not
          initiate TPT.
     - TPT Completion Rate: 5/6 = 83.33%
       → Below the 90% target. One patient did not complete treatment.
          This warrants investigation into reasons for non-completion.

     Central Delhi (District: Central Delhi / HF: GTB Hospital):
     - Contact Investigation Coverage: 55/60 = 91.67%
       → Below the 100% target. Five contacts were not evaluated, which
          in an absolute sense represents potential missed TB infection.
     - TPT Coverage: 49/55 = 89.09%
       → Just below the 90% target. Six eligible individuals did not
          initiate TPT.

     OVERALL INTERPRETATION:
     Both sites are performing close to but not yet at their targets. The
     most significant gap is in Contact Investigation Coverage, which has
     a 100% target — any uncovered contact represents a missed prevention
     opportunity. Programme managers should investigate whether the gaps
     are due to refusal, loss to tracking, or system lapses, and use the
     v_tpt_cascade_district view to identify exactly where patients are
     being lost at each step of the cascade.

DQ7. Compare the TST (Mantoux) and IGRA tests as modelled in the tbi_tests
     table. Discuss their respective positive cut-offs, sensitivity,
     specificity, cost, operational requirements, and the clinical situations
     in which each would be preferred in an Indian programmatic context.

A-DQ7.

     The database catalogues three TBI diagnostic tests: TST, QGIT (an
     IGRA), and T-SPOT.TB (another IGRA). Here is a detailed comparison:

     TST — Tuberculin Skin Test (Mantoux):
     - Positive cut-off: ≥5 mm in PLHIV; ≥10 mm in immunocompetent adults;
       ≥6 mm increase at follow-up.
     - Type: TST. Does NOT require a laboratory (requires_lab = FALSE).
     - Cost: Low.
     - Sensitivity: High.
     - Specificity: Low in BCG-vaccinated individuals (a significant
       limitation in India where BCG vaccination at birth is universal).
     - Operational notes: Requires an intradermal PPD injection, cold chain
       for tuberculin storage, and a trained reader at 48–72 hours. It is
       field-friendly once cold chain is maintained.
     - Preferred when: Resources are limited, laboratory capacity is absent
       (e.g., Sub-Centres, HWCs), or for large-scale programmatic screening
       in settings without IGRA access. Despite lower specificity in BCG-
       vaccinated populations, its low cost and no-lab requirement make it
       the default test in most public health facilities in India.

     QGIT — QuantiFERON-Gold In-Tube:
     - Positive cut-off: ≥0.35 IU/ml (ELISA-based reading).
     - Type: IGRA. Requires laboratory (requires_lab = TRUE).
     - Cost: High.
     - Sensitivity: High.
     - Specificity: High, even in BCG-vaccinated persons — a major
       advantage over TST in India.
     - Operational notes: Blood must reach the laboratory within 8–30 hours
       of collection. Requires ELISA reading capacity. Not affected by
       BCG vaccination, so false positives due to BCG are eliminated.
     - Preferred when: BCG vaccination history could confound TST results,
       in higher-resource facilities (Medical Colleges, Corporate Hospitals,
       ART Centres with IGRA capacity), or for immunocompromised patients
       where accurate TBI detection is critical. In the database, IGRA is
       available at KEM Hospital, GTB Hospital, SAT Hospital ART Centre,
       KGMU ART Centre, NIRT Chennai, Victoria Hospital, and Apollo
       Hospital Delhi — all higher-tier public or private institutions.

     T-SPOT.TB:
     - Positive cut-off: Spot count ≥6 on ESAT-6 or CFP-10 antigen plate.
     - Type: IGRA. Requires laboratory.
     - Cost: High.
     - Sensitivity: High.
     - An alternative IGRA measuring IFN-γ secreting T-lymphocytes.
       High specificity like QGIT, not affected by BCG.
     - Preferred in situations where QGIT is indeterminate or unavailable,
       or as an alternative in reference laboratories.

     PROGRAMMATIC CONTEXT IN INDIA:
     Under NTEP/PMTPT guidelines, TST is the primary TBI test at the field
     level due to its low cost and no laboratory requirement. IGRA (either
     QGIT or T-SPOT.TB) is recommended as a confirmatory or alternative test
     in facilities that have the capacity, particularly for PLHIV and
     immunocompromised patients where TST specificity is most problematic.
     The database accurately reflects this two-tier reality: most public
     facilities (HWCs, PHCs, Sub-Centres) have is_tbi_testing = TRUE but
     is_igra_available = FALSE, while Medical Colleges and ART Centres have
     both.

DQ8. Examine the treatment interruption management rules embedded in the
     database schema. For each of the four management actions recorded in
     the treatment_interruptions table, describe the clinical scenario in
     which each would apply, drawing on the treatment outcome definitions
     and regimen-specific LTFU thresholds.

A-DQ8.

     The treatment_interruptions table records each interruption event with
     fields for start date, end date, duration (auto-calculated in days),
     doses taken before interruption, percentage of doses taken, and the
     management_action chosen. The four allowed actions are:

     1. RESUME
        Clinical scenario: The patient interrupted TPT for a short period —
        below the LTFU threshold — and the interruption is now over. For 6H
        and 6Lfx, this means the gap was less than 8 consecutive weeks. For
        3HP and 4R, less than 4 consecutive weeks. Active TB has been ruled
        out. The patient can simply continue from where they left off, without
        restarting the full course, provided the total treatment duration does
        not exceed the extended duration (133% of the planned duration).
        Example: A 6H patient who missed 3 weeks due to travel resumes
        treatment on return.

     2. RESTART FULL COURSE
        Clinical scenario: The patient's gap exceeded the LTFU threshold
        (≥8 weeks for 6H/6Lfx or ≥4 weeks for 3HP/4R), but upon re-
        evaluation, they do not have active TB and are still willing and able
        to complete TPT. Rather than abandoning treatment entirely, the
        clinician decides a fresh full course is warranted — especially if
        a low number of doses were taken before the interruption (e.g.,
        <20% of planned doses). This ensures the patient receives an
        adequate protective course.
        Example: A 3HP patient who missed 6 weeks early in treatment (only
        2–3 doses taken) restarts the full 12-dose course.

     3. SWITCH REGIMEN
        Clinical scenario: The interruption was caused by an adverse event
        to the current regimen, or a new drug interaction has been identified
        that makes continuation of the original regimen unsafe or
        contraindicated. The patient is willing to continue TPT but on a
        different regimen. The alternative_regimen field in the
        adverse_event_reports table would also document this.
        Example: A patient on 3HP develops moderate flu-like hypersensitivity
        to Rifapentine; treatment is interrupted and then switched to 6H.
        Or: A 4R patient becomes pregnant — a contraindication for
        Rifampicin — so treatment is switched to 6H.

     4. DISCONTINUE
        Clinical scenario: Continuation of TPT in any form is no longer
        appropriate or safe. This may be because:
        (a) The patient developed confirmed active TB disease (outcome code:
            FAILED), requiring full TB treatment instead of preventive therapy.
        (b) A serious, life-threatening adverse event occurred (e.g.,
            drug-induced hepatitis, QTc prolongation) that precludes
            any further TPT.
        (c) The patient has exceeded the extended duration limit (133% of
            planned duration) and cannot be considered to have completed
            treatment, but restarting is also not appropriate.
        (d) The patient refuses further treatment after counselling.
        In all these cases, the enrolment outcome would be set to DISC_TOX,
        FAILED, DIED, or LTFU depending on the specific reason.
        Example: A 6Lfx patient develops Achilles tendon rupture — a Rare,
        stop_tpt = TRUE adverse event — and TPT is permanently discontinued.

     THE IMPORTANCE OF pct_doses_taken:
     The field pct_doses_taken in treatment_interruptions captures what
     fraction of doses had been taken before the interruption. This is
     critical for the Resume vs Restart decision: if the patient had already
     taken ≥80% (or ≥90% for 3HP) of doses before interrupting, they may
     qualify for "Treatment Completed" status regardless of the gap. If
     far fewer doses were taken, restarting is more clinically justified.

DQ9. Describe the role and structure of the three database views defined
     in the schema. For each view, explain the tables it joins, what
     information it surfaces, and give a practical example of how a district
     TB officer or programme manager would use it in day-to-day programme
     management.

A-DQ9.

     VIEW 1: v_active_tpt_enrolments
     Tables joined: tpt_enrolments, persons, tpt_regimens,
                    health_facilities, districts.
     Filter: Only enrolments where current_status = 'Active'.

     Information surfaced:
     For every patient currently on TPT, this view provides: their Nikshay
     ID, full name, age, sex, HIV status, the regimen code and name, start
     date, planned and extended end dates, weight, whether pyridoxine was
     prescribed, adherence method, the treating facility, and the district.
     All in a single, pre-joined row — no complex joins required by the user.

     Practical use:
     A District TB Officer (DTO) can run a simple SELECT on this view to get
     a real-time list of all patients currently on TPT in their district.
     They can filter by hiv_status to quickly identify all PLHIV on TPT,
     or by regimen_code to see how many patients are on 3HP vs 6H. A TBHV
     can check which patients are approaching their planned_end_date to
     schedule final counselling and outcome recording. The pyridoxine_
     prescribed flag helps ensure at-risk patients are not missed.

     ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     VIEW 2: v_tpt_cascade_district
     Tables joined: districts, persons, contacts, tb_screenings,
                    tbi_test_results, tpt_eligibility_assessments,
                    tpt_enrolments.

     Information surfaced:
     For each district, this view shows a stepwise care cascade count:
     - contacts_enumerated: How many contacts were identified.
     - contacts_screened: How many were screened for TB symptoms.
     - contacts_tbi_tested: How many received a TBI test.
     - contacts_eligible: How many were assessed as eligible for TPT.
     - contacts_initiated: How many actually started TPT.
     - contacts_completed: How many successfully completed TPT.

     Practical use:
     This is the cornerstone M&E tool. A programme manager can instantly
     see where the biggest drop-offs in the cascade occur in each district.
     For example, if contacts_screened is much lower than contacts_enumerated,
     the field team's reach is the bottleneck. If contacts_eligible is high
     but contacts_initiated is low, there is a counselling or drug supply
     problem. This view directly feeds into the CONT-INV-COV and TPT-COV
     indicator calculations. It can be used at monthly review meetings to
     hold district teams accountable and target corrective action.

     ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     VIEW 3: v_drug_interactions_summary
     Tables joined: drug_interactions (single table, no joins).

     Information surfaced:
     A clean, ordered lookup of all drug-drug interactions relevant to TPT:
     the TPT drug (Isoniazid or Rifamycin), the drug class of the
     interacting medication, the specific interacting drugs, the direction
     of interaction (Increases or Decreases), and the clinical note.
     Ordered by tpt_drug and drug_class for easy scanning.

     Practical use:
     A clinician at an ART Centre or DR-TB Centre about to initiate TPT
     in a patient on complex co-medications can query this view to quickly
     check whether any current medication interacts with the proposed TPT
     regimen. For example: "SELECT * FROM v_drug_interactions_summary
     WHERE tpt_drug = 'Rifamycin'" shows all 11 Rifamycin interactions
     at a glance — antiretrovirals, contraceptives, immunosuppressants,
     antifungals, and OST drugs — enabling an informed regimen choice
     before prescription. It can also be embedded in a clinical decision
     support screen of the Nikshay application.

DQ10. A programme evaluator reviewing the PMTPT database for Q1 2024
      notices that Suresh Patil completed 3HP with an outcome of "Treatment
      Completed," but his Month-1 follow-up adherence was recorded as only
      33.33%. Explain how this is clinically consistent, trace his full
      treatment timeline from the database, and describe what the database
      would record if he had instead been lost to follow-up.

A-DQ10.

      WHY 33.33% AT MONTH-1 IS NOT A RED FLAG:
      Suresh Patil is on the 3HP regimen, which has a total of 12 weekly
      doses over approximately 12 weeks (3 months). His enrolment started
      on 2024-01-26. His first follow-up visit was on 2024-02-26 — exactly
      one month later. At that point, only 4 of 12 planned weekly doses had
      been taken (4 ÷ 12 = 33.33%). This is arithmetically correct and
      entirely expected: in a weekly regimen, 4 weeks = 4 doses. The
      "33.33%" represents progress at the one-month mark, not a failure of
      adherence. By the second visit on 2024-03-26, he had taken 8 of 12
      doses (66.67%) — again consistent with 8 weeks having passed. He
      then completed all 12 doses by 2024-04-25 (the planned end date).
      Since 12/12 = 100%, which exceeds the 90% completion threshold for
      3HP, the outcome of "Treatment Completed" (outcome_id = 1) is fully
      justified.

      HIS FULL TREATMENT TIMELINE FROM THE DATABASE:

      2024-01-22  TBI test requested and administered (TST, Dharavi HWC).
      2024-01-24  TBI result: Positive (14 mm induration).
      2024-01-25  Eligibility assessment: HHC-5PLUS, active TB excluded,
                  3HP recommended (assessed by Dr Desai).
      2024-01-26  TPT enrolment: 3HP started. Weight: 72 kg. No pyridoxine.
                  Adherence method: 99DOTS. Planned end: 2024-04-25.
                  Extended end: 2024-06-03.
      2024-02-26  Month-1 follow-up: 4/12 doses (33.33%), weight 71.8 kg,
                  no symptoms, no AEs. Visited by TBHV Rakesh.
      2024-03-26  Month-2 follow-up: 8/12 doses (66.67%), weight 72.0 kg,
                  no symptoms, no AEs. Visited by TBHV Rakesh.
      2024-04-25  Treatment completed. Outcome recorded as 'Completed'
                  (outcome_id = 1, outcome_date = 2024-04-25).
                  enrolment status updated to 'Completed'.

      IF HE HAD BEEN LOST TO FOLLOW-UP INSTEAD:
      For the 3HP regimen, LTFU is defined as interruption for ≥4 consecutive
      weeks (≥28 days). Suppose Suresh had taken only 4 doses and then
      disappeared after his Month-1 visit. The following would be recorded:

      treatment_interruptions table:
      - interruption_start: 2024-02-26 (date of last dose or last contact)
      - interruption_end: NULL initially; filled in if he returns.
      - duration_days: computed when end date is entered.
      - reason: "Patient not contactable / missed doses"
      - doses_taken_before: 4
      - pct_doses_taken: 33.33%
      - management_action: 'Discontinue' if he never returns, or
        'Restart Full Course' if he returns after >4 weeks.

      tpt_enrolments table:
      - current_status would be updated to 'LTFU'
      - outcome_id would be set to the LTFU outcome type.
      - outcome_date: the date the LTFU decision is made.

      The monitoring indicator TPT-COMP (completion rate) for that period
      would record 1 fewer in the numerator, reducing the completion
      percentage for the facility. The v_tpt_cascade_district view would
      show contacts_initiated = 1 but contacts_completed = 0 for the
      cohort including Suresh.

      The POST-TPT-FU indicator (6m, 12m, 18m, 24m follow-up) would not
      be triggered at all, as follow-up is only scheduled for those who
      completed TPT — a patient with LTFU outcome would not generate
      post-TPT follow-up visit records.
